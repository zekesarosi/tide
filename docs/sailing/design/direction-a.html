Direction A

Chartplotter Pro

An instrument panel first and an app second. It follows the conventions of Garmin, B&G and Raymarine plotters, so a skipper who already owns one finds everything at once. A fixed chrome frame holds a full-bleed chart and a strip of six data tiles that you can reassign. Every control is a hard-edged soft key. The night-red mode is not a dark theme: every pixel shifts to the red end, so the helm keeps its night vision.

Typography

6.4 333° 32.1
SOG · COG · DEPTH

  • Barlow Condensed 600 for every number. Tabular figures, 50 pt in tiles and 78 pt for the alarm distance.
  • Barlow 600 caps with +12% tracking for labels and soft keys. It never drops below 11 pt.
  • Labels are all caps, like the abbreviations on a plotter. Plain-language sentences appear only in banners.

Iconography

anchorwindcurrentrouteaismoblayersbelldepthcompass

Square-capped, mitred, 2.2 px strokes on a 24 grid, made to read like plotter key legends. The chart uses IHO S-52 conventions: a magenta route, a red can or green cone for buoys, a magenta light flare, and a filled AIS danger triangle.

Sunlight readability

  • The day mode keeps the chart white and the chrome black. Glare-washed screens still hold more than 15:1 at the tiles.
  • Numbers never sit on the chart. They live in opaque tiles.
  • Every alert is coded twice, by color and by an outline, and the danger outline is 2 px.
  • Brightness and mode cycle from the status bar (day → dusk → night red), with automatic switching at civil twilight.

Wet and gloved hands

  • Soft keys are 78×60 pt, rail keys 56×56 pt and alarm actions 64 pt tall, all with at least 8 pt gaps.
  • No swipes are needed underway. Everything is a single tap or a press-and-hold.
  • Destructive actions (disarm, delete route) require a 2 s hold with a progress ring.
  • Holding the Action button gives MOB, which works from the Lock Screen.
